Appendix: Evidence Review

This appendix rates the published research behind each element of SELweb's theory of change. It is a reference, not a verdict. Most early-stage programs have strong evidence for some elements and none yet for others, and each gap marks a place where measurement would add the most. The Bears on column names the priority or section each row informs.

How to Read the Ratings

Evidence tier. Very strong: two or more well-conducted randomized or quasi-experimental studies with positive effects, at least one independent of the developer and none of comparable quality pointing the other way, or a positive What Works Clearinghouse rating. Strong: one such study, or a meta-analysis whose causal studies show benefit. Correlational: consistent associations across studies. Rationale only: a logic model or evidence for the mechanism, without outcome evidence. No evidence found: searched, nothing credible.

Flags. Inconsistent: studies of comparable quality disagree. Harm signal: a credible causal study found a negative effect in a comparable setting. Harm risk: a plausible harm inferred from a different setting or population.

Directness. Direct: evidence on SELweb itself. Same practice: the same practice in a similar population. Analogy: a related practice, or a different population or outcome.

Harm Signals and Risks

Harm signal: interim assessment in K–2. The Indiana school-randomized trial found significant negative effects on K–2 achievement (Konstantopoulos et al., 2016). The outcome was academic, so it does not transfer directly to SEL, but SELweb's K–3 edition serves the same grade band. Bears on P5.

Harm risk: grouping. If crosswalks or PD lead teachers to group low-scoring students together, peer contagion is possible: in small-group aggression prevention, children's aggression moved toward their group's average (Boxer et al., 2005). A broader meta-analytic review did not support iatrogenic group effects (Weiss et al., 2005), so this is a risk to watch, not a finding. Bears on P3.

Harm risk: labeling. Diagnostic labels lower adults' evaluations of students (meta-analysis of 60 experiments, g = −0.42; Franz et al., 2023). SELweb scores are not diagnoses, and the 6–8 validation paper says they should not drive high-stakes placement; report language and PD can reinforce that. Bears on P2.

Harm risk: group comparisons. K–3 scores reach only partial scalar invariance by ethnicity, so comparing group averages on reports could mislead. Bears on P2.

Harm risk: low-readiness schools. In the ANet trial, schools with low readiness for data use saw negative achievement effects, while high-readiness schools saw positive ones. Bears on P4 and P5.

How This Review Was Done

Each element was checked first against clearinghouses and practice guides (What Works Clearinghouse, Evidence for ESSA, Education Endowment Foundation), then against meta-analyses and individual trials, with separate searches for null and negative results. Quasi-experimental studies count equally with randomized trials when they establish baseline equivalence and handle attrition. A second, independent pass checked 21 key claims against their sources; two were corrected and four qualified before inclusion. About a third of sources were read in full text and the rest from abstracts or official summaries. Some publisher pages could not be accessed, and effect sizes for a few trials were not retrievable. All SELweb validation studies to date are by the developer.
