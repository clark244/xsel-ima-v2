Key Findings

Evidence at both ends, nothing in the middle.
Three federal measurement awards and ten peer-reviewed papers establish that SELweb measures what it claims. Nothing establishes whether teachers open the reports, understand them, and change instruction.
The instrument is not the intervention.
What would have to be evaluated is whether and how educators use the assessment reports, whether and how much that influences instruction, and whether those instructional decisions influence teacher and student success.
The organization has been responsive to feedback, when articulated.
Using feedback, the team made changes to the assessment. A teacher who opens a report but does nothing with it is harder to see, which is why the impact of SELweb reports on instruction and outcomes has stayed invisible.
Two of the three inputs for a credible study are in hand.
The comparison variation exists inside the customer base, and the outcome measure exists in-house. What is missing is a measure of teacher behavior between fall and spring assessments.

About xSEL Labs

xSEL Labs offers SELweb, a direct assessment of children's social-emotional skills in three grade-banded editions: children complete the tasks themselves rather than being rated by a teacher. Districts buy it; teachers receive norm-referenced reports on their classes and individual students, and curriculum crosswalks and professional development are intended to turn those reports into changed instruction. This assessment covers that chain.

Model Description

ElementDescription
ProductSELweb — a web-based, performance-based benchmark assessment of social and emotional skills, in three editions: Early Elementary (K–3), Late Elementary (4–6), and Middle School (6–8). Four skills are measured: emotion recognition, perspective-taking, social problem-solving, and self-regulation.
Implementation ContextWhole-class administration during the school day once in the fall and once in the spring. Students work independently with headphones, and instructions are embedded in the assessment so that non-readers can complete it. Roughly 30 minutes. Results are reported against national norms at student, classroom, grade, school, and district level.
User / Population ContextK–8 classroom teachers, principals, and district administrators. Purchase decisions are typically made by heads of student services or of curriculum and instruction. Districts run established SEL curricula, with at least a dozen crosswalked to SELweb.
User BehaviorThe behavior the model depends on: a teacher opens and reviews the class report, understands it, uses the curriculum crosswalk to locate the units that teach the weakest skill, and adjusts unit emphasis, pre-teaches, re-teaches, or coaches skills — without breaking the curriculum's scope and sequence. A parallel expectation is that teachers who act on the data come to feel more capable in their SEL instruction.
Mechanism 1How report review, the crosswalk, and professional development combine to identify a specific instructional target. This is the step that converts a score into a lesson decision. It has no operational definition today, and the crosswalk — the artifact that does the converting — is distributed by request with no record of who receives it.
Mechanism 2How targeted skill instruction develops the four measured skills. The underlying claim is well supported in the SEL literature and in xSEL Labs' own criterion work, but it has not been observed among SELweb users, because the instructional change upstream of it has not been measured.
Intended OutcomeImproved student social and emotional skills, and through them better peer relationships, less conflict-driven disruption, recovered instructional time, and stronger academic outcomes. xSEL Labs' own research establishes that SELweb scores are associated with classroom behavior and academic achievement across all three editions.

Evidence Landscape

Before identifying measurement opportunities, it is worth cataloging the evidence xSEL Labs already has.

Evidence AssetWhat It ShowsLimitations
Three IES measurement awards, 2011–2024SourceR305A110143 ($2,325,731); R305A160053 ($1,399,997); R305A200220 ($1,398,404)
 Thirteen consecutive years of federal measurement funding, with McKown as principal investigator throughout. Each award produced a validated instrument and peer-reviewed publications, delivered on schedule. All three are Measurement-goal awards funding assessment development and validation, not whether using the assessment leads to improved instruction and student outcomes.
Peer-reviewed psychometric corpusSourceTen papers, 2016–2025, in Psychological Assessment, Assessment, Social Development, Autism Research, Journal of Psychoeducational Assessment, and others
 Score reliability, factor structure, and item-level performance for all three editions. Measurement invariance across gender, ethnicity, time, and English and Spanish — meaning a score carries the same meaning for different groups of students, which most SEL measures cannot demonstrate. Addresses the properties of the instrument. No paper examines what educators do with the results.
Criterion validity evidenceSourceReported within the EE, LE, and MS validation papers, and in McKown, Russo-Ponsaran, Allen & Warren (2016), Infant and Child Development, 25, 119–136
 SELweb scores are associated with behavioral and academic outcomes across all three editions. The association with reading achievement holds controlling for IQ and is mediated by positive classroom behavior. Establishes that the skills matter. Does not establish that assessing them changes instruction, which is the step in between.
Shortened SELweb EE — the shipping formSourceMcKown, Kharitonova, Russo-Ponsaran & Aksu-Dunya (2023), Assessment, 30, 171–189
 A shortened form that saves substantial administration time for a modest cost in score reliability. It is a documented answer to the objection districts actually raise — whether teachers will think it is worth the time. The Spanish adaptation, the autism-population study, and the two full-length EE papers validated the predecessor instrument rather than the shipping form.
Special-population and Spanish-language validationsSourceRusso-Ponsaran, Lerner, McKown et al. (2019), Autism Research, 12, 1260–1271; Russo, McKown, Russo-Ponsaran & Allen (2018), Psychological Assessment, 30, 416–421
 Validity evidence in high-functioning autistic elementary students and in a Spanish-language adaptation — reach into two populations that most SEL measures do not address at all. Neither population appears in the product's positioning, case studies, or sales materials.
District case studiesSourceFive published studies: LAUSD (Spring 2022), Lancaster SD, Distinctive Schools, Mineola UFSD, and Glencoe District 35
 Documented adoption and implementation across varied district contexts, and one professional learning community at LAUSD using report data to guide SEL practice — the closest existing example of the intended teacher behavior. Four of the five are adoption narratives. The LAUSD outcome claim is an uncontrolled pre/post on xSEL Labs' own instrument, inside a highly resourced professional learning community.
Platform login dataSourceSELweb platform — most-recent-login field
 Confirms that accounts are accessed. A single mutable field, not an event log. There is no record of report views, time spent, or crosswalk requests, and no history to analyze retrospectively. Every question downstream of login requires additional data.

Impact Measurement Opportunities

A central finding of this analysis is that xSEL Labs faces one measurement problem rather than several, and that it sits in a specific place: between the moment a teacher opens a report and the moment instruction changes. Everything upstream of that moment is well evidenced. Everything downstream depends on it.

The Five Priorities

The priorities are ordered by dependency, not by schedule. Priorities 1, 2 and 3 begin immediately and run alongside each other; Priority 4 is gated on Priority 1 and delivers later. Priority 5 is gated on Priority 4 and delivers last.

1 Define the Intervention — What Is Actually Being Evaluated Now ○○●○ ▼
START HERE

SELweb is an assessment. The goal of SELweb is to support effective teaching and student learning, which is an intervention goal. The first goal, therefore, is to specify the chain of measurable events that have to happen, in what order, and in what manner for the fall assessment reports to influence teaching and learning.

What this involves

  • A written description of the composite intervention: what the teacher receives, what the teacher is expected to do, and in what sequence
  • A dosage definition — how often report review and instructional adjustment must occur for the intervention to count as delivered
  • A fidelity standard specifying the difference between delivered and not delivered, usable by an observer
  • A decision on whether the crosswalk is part of the intervention or a support material — the founder's account says the former, the product treats it as the latter
  • A decision on the unit of delivery — the individual teacher, or a school team such as the LAUSD professional learning community — since it determines whom Priority 3 samples and how Priority 5 is analyzed

This is a specification exercise rather than a data exercise, and it is inexpensive. It comes first because it determines what Priority 3 asks and what Priority 4 logs, and because logging is now an engineering build rather than a database query, specifying it correctly the first time is worth real money. The LAUSD professional learning community is the nearest existing example of what a defined, dosable version looks like in practice.

2 Report Comprehension — Test the Reports with Teachers Now ○●○● ▼
FASTEST EVIDENCE

In think-aloud sessions, a small sample of teachers works through sample class and student reports, saying what they see and what they would do next. Misreadings show up directly, without relying on teachers to judge their own understanding, and the findings feed report redesign immediately. No customers, data agreements, or engineering are required.

What to measure

  • Whether teachers correctly identify the class's relative strengths and weakest skill from a sample report
  • Whether they read norm-referenced scores correctly at student and class level
  • What instructional step they would take next, and whether they reach for the crosswalk unprompted
  • Which report elements confuse teachers or go unused

Eight to ten teachers across the three grade bands is usually enough to surface the main problems. It runs alongside Priority 1, because what teachers can take from a report bounds what the intervention can reasonably ask of them, and any report fix made now improves every measure that follows.

3 Teacher Understanding and Instructional Change — Ask Teachers Now Now ●●●○ ▼
HIGHEST VALUE

The aim of this priority is to establish three things. First, whether the teacher received a crosswalk for their curriculum — a teacher without one cannot have failed at the application step. Second, whether the teacher can read the report correctly, checked against their own class's scores rather than by asking how confident they feel. Third, what specifically changed, named against the curriculum rather than answered yes or no.

What to measure

  • Crosswalk receipt — which teachers have one, for which curriculum, and how it reached them
  • Report comprehension in practice — a short check against the teacher's own class data, informed by what Priority 2 finds
  • Specific instructional change — units re-sequenced, skills re-taught, real-time coaching, named against the curriculum
  • Whether teachers judge the reports worth the time — the second question district buyers ask
  • Whether acting on the data leaves teachers feeling more capable in their SEL instruction
  • Non-users and lapsed users, sampled deliberately alongside engaged teachers
4 Report and Crosswalk Telemetry to Make the Answer Continuous 6–18 months ○○●● ▼
DURABLE INFRASTRUCTURE

The platform currently stores a single most-recent-login field. It does not record whether a report was opened, which levels or sections were viewed, how long was spent, or whether a crosswalk was ever requested. Building that record converts a one-time study into standing data: Priority 3 answers the question once for a sample, and Priority 4 answers it continuously for the entire customer base.

What to measure

  • Report view events — timestamp, level viewed, and section-level interaction
  • Crosswalk distribution — who requests, for which curriculum, and whether it reaches classroom teachers or stops with administrators
  • A short in-platform prompt after report view, asking what was used, what changed, and whether the report was worth the time, joined to professional development attendance

Priority 4 is the only item here that is a product roadmap item and it should carry a named owner and a date.

5 Implementation and Student Growth–Testing the Full Chain 12–24 months ●○●○ ▼
FULL-CHAIN TEST

Priorities 3 and 4 establish what teachers do. This one connects it to what happens to students. It is gated on Priority 4, which supplies the implementation measure, and on Priority 1, which defines what “used the results” means precisely enough to be a variable. It is also the buyer's stated bar — improvement among users — which makes it the first priority whose result speaks directly to the district question rather than to internal diagnosis.

What this involves

  • Using implementation measures developed in Priority 4, and SELweb fall and spring scores
  • Examining the association between teacher implementation and student spring SELweb scores, controlling for fall SELweb scores
  • Using a multi-level hierarchical linear model to account for the nesting of students in classrooms and schools

The correlational result is an internally consistent test of the model, not an efficacy claim — the outcome is xSEL Labs' own instrument. Framed that way it is credible, and it is the evidence that makes an external-outcome study fundable.

What Changes If You Do This

If xSEL Labs executes against these priorities, it will have regular visibility into whether teachers view SELweb reports, understand them, and use them to guide instruction, and whether teachers find that worth their time. Knowing which of those steps breaks down tells the team where to improve the reports, the crosswalks, and the professional development that supports them. And because implementation and outcomes will be measured on the same students, xSEL Labs will be able to ask the question its model has always implied but has never tested: whether classes whose teachers used the results gained more than classes whose teachers did not. This information can inform product design, professional development design, and which customers need additional support to implement SELweb in ways that are maximally beneficial.

Recommended Next Steps

The five priorities imply a specific set of near-term actions. The first three can begin immediately and require no engineering. The last two prepare the platform work and the full-chain test so that they are specified before they are built.

Immediate

1
Draft the intervention specification. Name the composite — assessment, crosswalk, professional development, instructional adjustment — and define its dosage and fidelity. One document, and everything else depends on it.
2
Scope the teacher inquiry. Define the sample — including non-users and lapsed users — and commission the implementation-research design expertise the study needs.
3
Run the report think-alouds. Recruit eight to ten teachers across the three grade bands, test whether they read sample reports correctly, and feed what they misread into report design and the intervention specification.

Before the platform build

4
Inventory the crosswalks. Establish which curricula are covered and how requests are currently received, since this is the only distribution channel for the artifact the model depends on.
5
Specify the telemetry against the model. Once the intervention specification exists, write the logging requirement from it — what event, at what grain, answering which link in the chain, and keyed so it can be joined to fall and spring scores at the class and teacher level — and take that to the product roadmap with an owner and a date.

About This Report

This report has two purposes: first, to articulate a working model of the mechanisms through which SELweb is intended to produce impact; and second, to identify where measurement would most improve xSEL Labs' ability to understand and demonstrate that impact.

Our aim is to help xSEL Labs identify opportunities to better measure the intended impact of the assessments on teaching and learning.
